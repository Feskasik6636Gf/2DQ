# liars - "In this world, every lie you tell crawls out of your mouth as a monster."
Every lie you tell crawls out of your mouth as a monster. Say, "I love your haircut," and a moth-sized creature bites you. Nobody lies for long here. You can't hide a monster.
Except my dad. He has never had one. People call him Honest Ben. I'm Mia. I'm 11, and I have never heard him fib.
Then one night, something started tearing through town. It was the size of a barn. Sixty feet of fur and teeth. It flattened the bakery. It swallowed the mayor's car. At dawn, it vanished.
Whose lie could be that big? "Whoever owns that beast, confess now!" the mayor shouted. Nobody spoke.
So my brother Sam and I followed the claw marks. They stopped at our cellar door. We opened it. Inside, curled around the furnace, was the monster. And there was Dad, holding out a bowl of food. "Hello, old friend." He saw us and went pale.
"How long?" I asked. "Nine years." Nine years. Since Mom. "When people ask where she is, I say she's traveling," he whispered. "And it grows."
My throat burned. Mom wasn't traveling. Mom was gone. "Dad, say it." "I can't." The monster opened one yellow eye. Outside, sirens. The town was coming with torches. "Please," I said.
Dad knelt. His voice cracked. "Your mother died."
The monster shuddered. It shrank. It melted into smoke and drifted up like a sigh. Dad pulled us close, and we cried until the sirens faded.
Then he wiped his eyes and asked, "Are you two okay?" I smiled. "I'm fine."
Behind me, something huge opened its eyes.
